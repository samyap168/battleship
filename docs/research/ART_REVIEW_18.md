# Armada Ascension — Art Review 18

All 35 shots reviewed at pixel level against the eight claims made since Review 17. Six of the eight land at least partially, and two of Review 17's own complaints turn out to have been resolved for reasons the code makes clear: the karst tri-tone fix (Review 17's #1) is now visible in the two dedicated close shots, and the Age-5 chromatic-aberration "nameplate fringing" (Review 17's #2) was a misdiagnosis — `hud.js` draws nameplates on a separate 2D canvas the WebGL post-process chain can't reach, and no fringing shows up on any nameplate sampled this pass. What's left of that CA issue is a faint, low-severity fringe on scene silhouettes only. One new, genuine bug turned up: the "LEVEL n" level-up callout clips off the bottom edge of the frame at 1280×650. And the staged captain-sinking cinematic — confirmed intact in code and visibly firing elsewhere in this same gallery (`25`, `34`) — simply wasn't what got captured under the `28`/`29` filenames this run, which reads as a gallery-timing gap rather than an art regression.

## Scores

**Visual Quality: 9/10.** The three set-piece art claims this cycle are real improvements, not just tuning. Trade ports (`32_port_close`) now read as working harbour towns — timber-frame studs, warm-lit windows with sills, a loft door, a hoist beam and crane, a stone chimney stack — a genuine step up from the "toy blocks" `structureModels.js` used to ship. Shore boulders (`15`, `22`, `32`) are confirmed smooth-shaded (welded icosahedra, `computeVertexNormals`, no flat facets) with a real wet-waterline/dry-crown/moss paint pass, and it shows on screen as convincing rounded stone rather than low-poly rubble. Shell splashes (`33_splash_sideon`) are now unmistakably a narrow column with a crown of droplets, not the cotton-ball blob flagged in earlier reviews. Karst tri-tone (Review 17's #1) is real progress but still incomplete: it's clearly visible in the two dedicated close shots (`30`, `31`) but every wide establishing shot in this gallery (`00`, `01`, `13`, `18`, `22`) still reads as one warm-grey palette, because backlight and fog still dominate before the per-stack tint does. Held below a 10 by that unfinished wide-shot legibility and by the new 1280×650 clipping bug below.

**VFX Readability & Impact: 9/10.** The splash fix (`33`) and the doubled storm rain (`09_storm`, `RAIN_N` 2600→5200, confirmed in `weather.js` and its git history) both land cleanly on screen — the storm shot shows dense, slanted, colour-neutral streaks across the whole frame with no rainbow fringing, exactly as intended. Explosions, drone swarms and the Leviathan set-piece (`03`, `11`, `14`, `21`) all still read instantly. The one soft spot: the new "crippled captain trails a dark plume below 25% hull" tell (`game.js:754-761`) is confirmed firing in code but isn't visually distinguishable from ambient combat/spray smoke in the one shot that should showcase it (`34_crippled_ship`) — the list/lower-draft half of the damage tell reads great, the plume half doesn't yet.

**UI/UX: 9/10.** Up from 8. Nameplates keep their dark backing pill everywhere sampled (`03`, `08`, `16`, `17`, `23`) and stay legible over smoke, bloom and sun glint. Review 17's headline UI complaint — Age-5 chromatic aberration fringing nameplate text — does not reproduce anywhere in this gallery, including in `23_mothership_close`, the direct descendant of the shot that showed it last time; the nameplates for "Cochrane" and "de Ruyter" there are crisp white-on-dark with no colour separation, consistent with `hud.js` drawing them on a canvas the WebGL CA pass never touches. New ding, and a genuine one: `24_laptop_1280x650` shows the "LEVEL 4" (then "LEVEL 5") float-text callout clipped by the bottom edge of the frame (x:330-455, y:635-656) — a real responsive-layout bug at that aspect ratio, not a capture artifact.

**First-10-Seconds Wow: 9/10.** Unchanged — `00`, `01`, `06`, `13` remain marketing-key-art quality and none of this cycle's changes touch that specific beat.

**Gameplay Camera Readability: 9/10.** Unchanged. Faction rim light, nameplate legibility and reflection breakup all still read cleanly in combat and orbit shots (`08`, `16`, `19`, `20`); nothing regressed.

## Top 6 fixes, ranked by visual impact per hour of work

1. **"LEVEL n" callout clips off the bottom edge at 1280×650 (`24_laptop_1280x650`, x:330-455, y:635-656).** `game.js:123` spawns the float text at a fixed world-space offset (`h.x, 16, h.z`) via `ui.floatText`, with no on-screen safe-margin clamp before its upward drift animation starts. At a wide/short aspect ratio the text can spawn already touching the bottom edge. A one-line clamp on the projected spawn `y` (keep it ≥40px from any edge) fixes a real, screenshotable bug for the price of a bounds check.
2. **Port test framing hides 3 of the 5 cottages `structureModels.js:662-666` places.** The warehouse dressing (timber, windows, loft door, hoist, chimney) reads great in `32_port_close`, but the camera is close enough that the cottages that are supposed to sell scale are mostly occluded or off-frame. Pull the shot back ~15% or nudge the angle so at least a cluster of cottages is visible — the asset work is already done, it just isn't earning credit on screen.
3. **Crippled-captain plume (`game.js:754-761`) is invisible against ambient combat smoke.** It fires correctly at hp<25%, but in the one shot built to show it (`34_crippled_ship`) it's indistinguishable from the general mist/cannon smoke already in frame. Give it a distinct cool/dark tint and a steeper, more vertical trajectory than the ambient smoke so it reads as "this specific ship is dying" rather than blending into the fight.
4. **Karst tri-tone still washes out in every wide shot (`00`, `01`, `13`, `18`, `22`).** The fix (`environment.js:335-358`) genuinely works at close range (`30`, `31`) — the value steps are real. But backlight and fog are still eating the tint before it reaches camera in every establishing shot. Widen `tintR`/`tintB` further apart and/or boost stoneKind 3's (pale cream) exposure specifically, since that's the variant most likely to punch through backlit haze.
5. **Age-5 CA still fringes scene silhouettes faintly at frame corners.** `main.js:433` keeps `ca: 0.0013` unchanged from Review 17; nameplates are now proven immune (separate canvas), but a soft red/cyan edge is still visible on the top-right karst spire in `23_mothership_close` (x:1400-1600, y:0-100). Since the bigger complaint (nameplate fringing) is resolved, this is now a small, cheap follow-up: drop the value to ~0.0009 as suggested twice before.
6. **The gallery capture doesn't reliably land on the staged sinking cinematic.** The sequence is confirmed working (`25`, `34` both show it firing), but the shots explicitly named for it (`28`, `29`) captured ordinary combat instead this run. Not an art bug, but hook the screenshot trigger directly to the `sinkFx` event start rather than a fixed timer/camera cue, so future review passes can actually verify this set-piece instead of a coin-flip capture.

## Broken

- **New, genuine:** the "LEVEL n" float-text callout clips off the bottom of the frame at 1280×650 (`24_laptop_1280x650`, top fix #1) — a real responsive-layout gap in the world-to-screen float-text system, not a capture artifact.
- **Resolved / non-issue, corrected from Review 17:** the Age-5 chromatic-aberration "nameplate fringing" flagged last review was a misdiagnosis, not a landed fix — `hud.js` draws nameplates on a separate 2D canvas the WebGL post-process chain cannot reach, and no fringing shows up on any nameplate sampled this pass (`03`, `08`, `16`, `17`, `23`). A faint, low-severity fringe does remain on scene-geometry silhouettes at frame corners in Age 5 (see top fix #5) — that one is real but minor, and unrelated to UI legibility.
- No other genuine rendering bugs found this pass. The `28`/`29` sinking-cinematic gap (see claim 8 and top fix #6) reads as a capture-timing issue, not a code or art defect — the feature fires correctly elsewhere in the same gallery.
