# Armada Ascension — Art Review 8

Reviewed all 17 images in `tests/output/gallery8/` against the same set in `gallery7` and the Round 7 write-up, with PIL pixel crops (column-wise RGB sampling on the flag, matched deterministic-frame diffs on the citadel beam, karst horizon and the 9:59 explosion), and a source diff of `src/render/environment.js`, `src/render/fx.js` and `src/render/models/materials.js` (commits `42e7437`, `2c6d2fa`) to check code claims against pixels before trusting them. SwiftShader noise, harness title cards, stale hints/"SUNK" overlays and the `01`/`12`/`13` letterbox are ignored per brief.

## Scores

**Visual Quality: 9.0/10 (+0.5).** The two headline claims both hold up under a matched-frame diff. The horizon karst in `01`, `06`(menu splash uses the same backdrop layer), `12`, `13` and `15` is a real, dramatic upgrade: side-by-side crops at identical pixel coordinates show Round 7's smooth pink-brown domes replaced by slim, jagged, height-varied Guilin/Ha Long spires (`materials.js`/`environment.js` diff: `karstTower(r*0.52, h*1.12, ...)` instead of the old `karstGeometry`). The citadel light column is equally confirmed: the same beam at the same pixel coordinates in `06_menu` goes from Round 7's crisp, constant-width rectangular pillar to a genuinely soft-edged, top-tapered shaft with a visible warm glow pooling at its base. Flags are real but marginal: a column-by-column RGB sample of the `05_age_choice` preview-card flag shows Round 7's near-flat brightness curve (155-159 across the whole flag) replaced by a clearly oscillating one in Round 8 (120-162), confirming the travelling-fold shader fires — but the flag is only ~13px wide on screen, so in every normal gameplay frame this reads as texture noise, not cloth (see Fix #3). Foliage is the one claimed fix that still under-delivers: matched-crop comparison of the same tree in `10_island` shows a modestly wider, slightly flatter crown, but the silhouette is still fundamentally a cluster-of-spheres, not silhouette-defined lobes.

**VFX Readability: 9.4/10 (+0.2).** The explosion smoke fix is the clean win of the round: the identical 9:59 explosion frame in `11_explosion` (deterministic, so a true apples-to-apples diff) shows Round 7's flat brown cap replaced by a smoke mass that is warm orange-brown through its lit upper body and cools only at the fringes — confirmed again in a fresh `04_battle_closeup` crop showing the same orange-core/white-cool-edge gradient from a different angle. Debris embers are code-confirmed brighter (`fx.js`: smouldering-edge emissive term `vec3(0.5,0.16,0.04)`→`vec3(0.7,0.22,0.05)`, band widened `0.55`→`0.45`) but the underlying complaint from Round 7 — 2-3px coverage per chunk — isn't addressed by a brightness change alone (Fix #6). Cooldown dimming and the wake gradient, both closed in Round 7, remain solid in `04`/`09`/`17`.

**UI/UX: 9.5/10 (+0.1).** No regressions anywhere in the panel chrome, cooldowns, or nameplates. The plateSheen keyframe genuinely changed (`style.css`: hold phase `0%,55%`→`0%,25%`, so the sweep now runs 75% of the 11s loop instead of 45%), which is real, verifiable progress on Round 7's exact ask — but it is still a claim I can't independently confirm from stills: seven single-frame samples across rounds have now failed to catch it moving, and this round's samples (`02`, `09` shop panels) are no exception, just two static plates with a faint diagonal sheen band frozen at different, plausible-looking positions. Taking the brief's word that it was verified via the Web Animations API, but the capture harness itself still can't prove it (Fix #8).

**First-10-Seconds Wow Factor: 9.5/10 (+0.4).** This is where the karst and beam fixes pay off hardest, because `01`, `06`, `12`, `13` and `15` are exactly the shots a store page or trailer would use, and all five got measurably better in the same pass. `06_menu`'s title-card backdrop in particular now reads as a genuine hero establishing shot — six soft light columns, slender misted spires receding to the horizon, reflections holding together — where Round 7's version had hard-edged beams and pudgy pink cones. The one thing holding this back from higher is that the near/far karst inconsistency (Fix #2) and the spire-tip glitch (Fix #5) are both most visible in precisely these hero frames.

## What's shippable vs. what's still holding it back

**Shippable now:** the horizon karst spires and the volumetric citadel beam, both confirmed by matched-pixel diff (`01`, `06`, `12`, `13`, `15`); the fire-lit-to-soot explosion smoke, confirmed on a deterministic frame (`11`, `04`); everything closed in prior rounds (Leviathan nameplate, castle reflection, wake gradient, cooldown dimming, event-banner/score-bar spacing — no collision found in `08` this round).

**Still holding it back:** foliage canopies that, on a second attempted fix, still read as round blobs rather than silhouette-defining lobes; a flag cloth-fold shader that is real in code and in a zoomed crop but invisible at the screen size flags actually render at in every normal shot; a karst system that looks like two different rock formations depending on distance to camera; and a small but repeatable geometry glitch at the tip of every karst spire.

## Top 8 Remaining Fixes (ranked by impact)

1. **Foliage canopies are still round-blob at normal view distance despite a second, real code fix.** `environment.js`'s crown radii only shrank to 1.65/1.35/1.05 (from 1.65-i*0.45) while the 6 new lobes only reach 1.25 max with a 0.6 y-scale — core and lobes are still the same order of magnitude, so the silhouette stays sphere-cluster. Visible in `10_island` (crop x:700-870,y:130-270, the isolated tree right of the palm). Recipe: cut the crown radii again to ~1.0/0.8/0.6, push lobe radius to 1.4-1.7 and flatten harder (`c.scale(1, 0.4, 1)` instead of 0.6), and jitter each lobe's Y-rotation by ±15° so they don't all sit at the same ring radius reading as a fringe.
2. **Karst radius-scale is tied to distance layer, not intrinsic variety — near islands are all wide mesas, horizon peaks are all slim spires, and the seam shows.** `environment.js` line ~400 spawns mid-ground islands with `karstTower(r, h, ...)` (full radius) while the new horizon pass at line ~556 uses `karstTower(r*0.52, h*1.12, ...)`. Both layers are visible together in `06_menu` (crop x:950-1600,y:0-350) and `13_opening_b`, where a cluster of fat dome-topped islands sits meters from a cluster of needle spires of supposedly the same rock. Recipe: give the near-island spawn loop the same `0.35-0.65` radius-scale range the horizon loop uses (randomised per tower, not per layer), so slim and wide towers intermix at every depth instead of flipping at a hard distance cutoff.
3. **The cloth-fold shader is real but band-limited so tightly it aliases into noise at the screen size flags actually render at.** `materials.js`'s `applyClothFolds` drives fold shading off `sin(cu.x*15.0 - uClothT*7.0 ...)` — 15 cycles across a UV span that covers roughly 12-15 screen pixels in every sampled shot (`05_age_choice`, `04_battle_closeup`, `17_zoomed_in_gameplay`), i.e. sub-pixel-per-cycle, which is exactly the kind of shimmer the brief tells us to ignore when SwiftShader does it — except this one is geometry-driven, not a rasterizer artifact, and will alias identically on real hardware. Recipe: drop the base frequency to ~5-6 and drive the fold amplitude by `fwidth(cu.x)` so it band-limits itself as the flag shrinks with distance, instead of just adding more unresolvable detail.
4. **Every flag in the game folds in lockstep because they all share one global time uniform.** `applyClothFolds` sets `sh.uniforms.uClothT = CLOUD.uCloudT` with no per-instance offset, so a fleet of ten ships will have every flag crest and trough at the exact same instant — a subtle but real "puppet" tell once you know to look for it (compare masthead flags across any multi-ship frame, e.g. `16_brawl_lowangle`). Recipe: add a per-instance phase offset seeded from world position, e.g. replace `uClothT` in the fold terms with `uClothT + dot(vWorldPos.xz, vec2(0.07, 0.11))`, computed in the vertex stage and passed down as a varying.
5. **Karst spire tips have a small jagged double-notch glitch breaking the smooth silhouette right at the crown apex.** Visible in `13_opening_b` (crop x:880-1000,y:190-280, right-hand spire) and again in `15_castle_reflection` (crop x:130-260,y:140-230) — a sharp one-or-two-vertex spike pokes above the otherwise smooth dome curve against open sky. Likely the domed-summit falloff (`rad *= sqrt(max(0, 1 - c*c*0.97))` in `karstTower`) interacting badly with the `y>0.98` crown-jitter displacement (`(fbm(...) - 0.3) * r * 0.25`) at the last one or two rings, producing an un-smoothed pole vertex. Recipe: clamp the crown-jitter term to rows below `y > 0.98` only when `rad` at that ring is still above a minimum threshold, or simply skip displacement on the topmost ring and let `computeVertexNormals` blend from the ring below.
6. **Debris embers are code-confirmed brighter but not code-confirmed bigger, so Round 7's actual complaint (2-3px of visible glow per chunk) likely still stands.** The Round 7 fix only raised the emissive constant (`vec3(0.5,0.16,0.04)`→`vec3(0.7,0.22,0.05)`, ~+40%) and widened the `smoothstep` band from `0.55` to `0.45`, a ~15% area gain at best — a brightness change, not a coverage change. Recipe: widen the band further (`smoothstep(0.25, 0.95, cr)`) and multiply `s0`/`s1` (ember sprite scale) directly rather than relying on the crack mask alone to carry more visible area.
7. **The beam's new bloom now visually merges with the reforging shield-dome effect into one indistinct white mass.** In `02_reforging`, the softer, wider-blooming citadel beam passes directly behind the player ship's own translucent white repair-dome, and both are similar-temperature near-white glows at similar screen positions — where Round 7's harder-edged beam read as a clearly separate background element, Round 8's softer one now competes with foreground gameplay VFX for the same visual real estate (crop x:600-1000,y:300-700). Recipe: tint the beam's hot-base term (`applySoftBeam`'s `1.0 + (1-smoothstep(...))*0.8`) toward cool cyan-white instead of neutral white so it reads as background/environmental against the dome's warmer gold rim-light, or fade the beam's alpha within a fixed radius of any active shield-dome instance.
8. **HUD sheen: code genuinely fixed the exact defect Round 7 named, but it remains unconfirmed in eight straight single-frame samples across two rounds.** The hold phase shrank from 55% to 25% of the 11s loop exactly as recommended, which should make it far more likely to be caught mid-sweep — yet the `02`/`09` Armory panel samples this round are, again, two static plates. This isn't a code problem anymore; it's a verification gap. Recipe: have the capture harness take two screenshots of the same live, unpaused match 2-3 real seconds apart (not two different matches, and not one frame per gallery slot) and diff them — that's the only way to close this out with pixels instead of trust.

## The one change to reach 9.5

**Fix the foliage canopy silhouette for real this time.** This is the third round in a row it's been flagged, the second round in a row the team has genuinely touched the code, and the second round in a row the on-screen result hasn't moved enough to matter — core and lobe radii are still the same order of magnitude, so a cluster of spheres stays a cluster of spheres no matter how the lobe count or offset angle is tuned. With the flags now real (if under-scaled) and the karst/beam pair delivering genuine trailer-quality hero shots, foliage is the last asset in nearly every ground-level and establishing shot that still looks like an early prototype next to everything around it. The fix is cheap — a bigger radius delta between core and lobe, a harder Y-flatten, some rotational jitter — the risk is doing the same small, code-real, screen-invisible pass a third time.

## Verdict

Round 8 is the strongest round since the reflection and nameplate fixes: two of the three headline claims (karst spires, volumetric beam) are not just real in source but visibly, dramatically better in matched-pixel comparisons, and they land in exactly the shots that do the most marketing and first-impression work for the game. The smoke fix is smaller but equally well-verified on a deterministic frame. The flag fix is honest, real, and still not enough — cloth folds that only resolve in an 8x crop aren't cloth folds at normal viewing distance, they're aliasing, and that's a more interesting problem than "unlit material" was. The team should bank the karst and beam wins, spend real effort on foliage a third time with a bigger structural change rather than another parameter nudge, and — separately from the art itself — fix the capture harness so HUD sheen can finally be confirmed or denied instead of taken on faith for a third straight round.
